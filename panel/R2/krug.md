# Critic: Usability (Krug) - Landing, round 2

Anchor: Don't Make Me Think (Steve Krug): the five-second test, and "get rid of half the words, then half of what's left".

**Verdict: OBJECT (mild).** In five seconds the page says what it is and gives me a button, which is most of the job. At both widths I can tell that "learn bash" is a place to learn the Bash shell, that the examples are real, and where to start. The headline, lede and two buttons are clear and sit high. The trouble is what follows: the big terminal window is a tall, clever lecture on pipes that a beginner has to decode. It does not block the button, but at 390px it adds 913px of scroll between the buttons and the first card. Nothing is broken. It is a good page with a tall decoration in the way of the navigation.

## Five-second test
**390x844 (fold shot + measured):** Could tell: "$ learn bash" (a shell lesson site), real examples, a green button "Start with the basics", a second outlined button. Could not tell: who it is for (beginners? admins?). The kicker "from first command to scripts" half-says it but is tiny and green on dark. The big funnel is unreadable as to purpose: numbers (40, 16, 16...) next to commands, no hint of why.
**1280x800:** Same, better. Headline, lede, both buttons and the top of the terminal all fit above 800px (buttons at y=365-413). The funnel shows numbers and shapes; I can see "40 to 3" but not why I care.
Could not tell at either width: that there is a tool that explains pasted commands (only the button label hints), and that commands are risk-marked (that is below the fold, in the band).

## Findings
1. **MAJOR - Page is 913px of terminal demo before the first card (390px).** Measured: buttons end y=442; terminal window y=467-1380; "Where to go" cards start y=1494, i.e. 1.8 screens down. A visitor who wants Commands or Resources (not "basics") has to scroll past a diagram they did not ask for, or open the hamburger-less nav, which is a wrapped text row at the top (works, but small). At 1280 the window is 558px, cards at y=1183, acceptable.
   Change: on phone, cap the demo (show first 3 stages plus the final output, or collapse behind a "See the pipeline" disclosure); or move the "Where to go" cards above the demo. Keep the full window on desktop.
2. **MAJOR - The graphic does not explain itself; the caption does the work.** Without the caption the viewer sees a green funnel, numbers and command names. "lines" is the only unit cue. The caption ("gets thinner as each command throws lines away...") sits BELOW the output, after the viewer has scrolled past the pipe, and at 390px is off the first screen entirely. Nobody reads it first. Also the explanation link "See it explained, step by step" is the real call to action for this block and it is buried in the caption's last line.
   Change: put one short line directly above the funnel: "Each step keeps fewer lines. Thickness = lines left." Make "See it explained" a visible button-like link under the output.
3. **MINOR/MAJOR - The question line is half jargon.** "Which pages are missing, and how often are they asked for?" is plain English and fine for a beginner, the best line in the block. But "(7 commands, joined by pipes)" assumes the reader knows what a pipe is, and the funnel is literally called "pipe" in the caption. A beginner has no idea that "joined by pipes" is the lesson. Change: "(7 small commands, each passing its result to the next)". Introduce "pipe" after the idea. Also the question is an English sentence typed after "$", which a newcomer may read as the command itself; it is a prompt, not bash. Consider a faint label "The question" and "The commands".
4. **MINOR - Primary button is below the thumb-easy zone on a 390x844 phone.** Button y=335-383, centre ~359 of 844: upper third, which is the stretch zone for one-handed use; the second button (394-442) is better but is the secondary action. Mitigation: both buttons are full-width-ish blocks 48px tall, good targets; the page scrolls, so this is not a blocker. Change: none needed if finding 1 is fixed; optional sticky "Start" is overkill.
5. **MINOR - Buttons are clearly buttons.** Primary is solid green with dark text and glow; secondary is an outlined green pill with 48px height and 16px text. Both read as clickable. One quibble: the ghost button's outline is the same green as non-clickable decoration (window dots, kicker, numbers), so green stops meaning "press me". Change: keep green for buttons only; make numbers and kicker a quieter tone.
6. **MINOR - Headline reads fine; the cursor is a good joke that costs nothing.** "$ learn bash" in huge mono type, readable at both widths; the block cursor is clear as a cursor. But the header brand says "learn-bash_" (hyphen, underscore cursor) and the H1 says "learn bash" with a block: two cursors, two spellings, five centimetres apart. Change: one cursor, one spelling (drop the cursor in the brand).
7. **MINOR - Animation.** 9 animated elements measured. The blink is near the lede on phone (the headline is directly above the lede). A blinking block at 17px distance from reading text pulls the eye. Change: stop blinking after ~5 cycles or blink only on hover/focus; honour prefers-reduced-motion (verify it does; I did not test).
8. **MINOR - Kicker is too small and too low-contrast to count as the "who is it for" line.** It is the only place that says "first command to scripts". Change: promote it into the lede: "Learn Bash from your first command to scripts. Every example was run for real."

## Compare with a quieter alternative
Plain version: H1, the same lede, two buttons, then the six cards at once (cards at ~y=500 on phone instead of 1494). I would lose: the single most convincing proof of "real output" (the pipe with measured line counts is memorable and truthful), and the personality that makes this site not look like the manual. I would not lose: orientation, because the headline and buttons already do that work. The demo is worth keeping as the second screen, not as the thing that stands between me and the menu. Blind spot declared: I favour the familiar; the funnel is strange on purpose and may be what people remember.

## What needs a real phone
- Whether the 48px buttons are reached one-handed on a 6"+ device with the browser's bottom bar taking the lower ~80px; my numbers are CSS pixels in a desktop Chromium.
- Whether the glow and the blinking block are legible in sunlight and on OLED at low brightness.
- Real scroll feel and whether the funnel's tall shape at 390px feels like a swipe-trap.
- Whether the nav row of five words wraps or truncates on 360px and 320px devices (I measured only 390 and 1280).
- Whether the animation janks on a mid-range phone and what it does with reduced-motion on.
